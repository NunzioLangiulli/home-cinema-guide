# Home Cinema — A wiki and settings guide for watching films at home

**Read it as a website:** [nunziolangiulli.github.io/home-cinema-guide](https://nunziolangiulli.github.io/home-cinema-guide/) (search and side menu).

> Understand **what** to set on your TV, **why**, and **where** certain conventions come from (24 fps, D65, gamma 2.4, black bars, Atmos…), with every claim linked to its source.

The starting point is a 2026 video series by the YouTube channel **Slidebean** on picture and sound quality at home, plus their free *TV Calibrator* app. The videos explain complex topics clearly and give practical fixes. This guide builds on them and **checks, corrects or extends** each claim with primary sources: ITU/SMPTE standards, UHD Alliance documents, RTINGS measurements and manufacturer documentation.

## How to read the reliability labels

Every important claim carries one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Verified** | Confirmed by a primary source (standard, official document, independent measurement) |
| ⚠️ **Simplified** | Essentially true but imprecise or incomplete; the note explains what is missing |
| ❌ **Incorrect** | Contradicted by primary sources; the note gives the correct version |
| ❓ **Unverified** | Plausible, but we found no reliable source; treat with caution |
| 💬 **Opinion** | Aesthetic preference or judgement, not a fact |
| ⏳ **Pending** | Not true yet but expected (e.g. a repository announced but not published); to be rechecked |

## Guiding principle

There is no single "right setting". There is a **reference** (how the film was approved in the grading suite: D65 white, standard gamma/EOTF, no added processing) and there are **justified deviations** from it (a bright room, panel limits, personal taste). This guide helps you know *when* you are deviating and *why*; it does not forbid it.
